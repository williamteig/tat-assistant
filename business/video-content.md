# Video Content — Vimeo Library Summary

A summary of all video content in the TAT Vimeo library, organised by folder. This document describes what is covered in each folder and each video, based on the downloaded transcripts.

## Insta Lives

Workshop-style sessions originally broadcast live on Instagram. Content focuses on audition technique, character identity, and self-taping — teaching actors to break away from safe, predictable choices and embrace creative risk-taking. The emphasis is on individuality, bold character creation, and active career ownership.

- **Actor Online Workshop March 2023** — Explores how auditions should showcase individuality rather than technical correctness, using the analogy of architects creating distinctive buildings; teaches actors to find their unique character identity.
- **In Self Tapes Which Take Do You Choose (Teaser)** — A brief teaser addressing the practical challenge of selecting which take to submit when self-taping multiple versions of a scene.
- **Online Audition Workshop - Actor Audition Limits Are Stopping Your Career** — Discusses how actors limit themselves by playing it safe; emphasises breaking boundaries and challenging expected character choices.
- **Online Audition Workshop - CD Workshops. How To Make Them Work for You** — Strategies for casting director workshops including preparation, engagement tactics, and post-workshop follow-up to create lasting impressions.
- **Online Audition Workshop - You Can Self Tape Anything... So What Should You Choose?** — Explains how self-taping is a creative exercise; encourages actors to choose stock characters and develop distinctive character territories.
- **Online Workshop - How to Get in Front of a Camera** — Encourages actors to get comfortable on camera through experimentation and self-created content, using examples from successful streaming series to illustrate the power of unique character identity.
- **Self Tapes 2.0 - An Update from Greg Apps** — Introduces the concept of Self Taping 2.0, where actors shift from reactive (waiting for auditions) to proactive (creating their own content) to establish character identity.
- **Self Taping 2.0 - What it is, Why You Need It** — Comprehensive explanation of how self-taping 2.0 empowers actors to create characters on their own terms, becoming active creators rather than passive audition responders.
- **Should Auditions Be Paid (Teaser)** — A brief introduction to the debate around paid auditions and the considerations for actors regarding fair compensation.
- **The Audition Technique Workshop - Audition Fearlessly** — Teaches actors to embrace indecision and unpredictability in auditions, moving away from locked-in choices and fear-based preparation.

## Livestreams

Real-time educational sessions addressing specific audition challenges and actor mindsets. Topics tackle punctuation's impact on delivery, the problem of indicating, over-preparation, and rediscovering the joy of acting. The overall message is to trust instinct, remove technical restrictions, and understand what casting directors actually want.

- **New Year Livestream (01/01/26)** — Introduces "emotional archaeology" as a method for finding depth in simple dialogue words; discusses how punctuation in scripts can restrict creative freedom.
- **14th Livestream - How to Get the Joy of Acting Back** — Addresses how successful actors focus on character creation rather than success/failure outcomes; advocates for enthusiasm about character development as the true measure of self-worth.
- **16-11 Livestream - What They Don't Teach You in Acting** — Examines how indicating (overtly showing emotions) undermines credibility with casting directors; advocates for restraint and pursuing objectives rather than demonstrating feelings.
- **Does Punctuation Help or Hinder Your Audition? (Feb 22)** — Explores how slavish adherence to script punctuation creates predictable performances, and how trusting human spontaneity creates more engaging auditions.
- **The Key To Booking? Audition Recklessly! (Nov 2025)** — Teaches actors to manage risk rather than seek perfection, experiment constantly, and maintain excitement about opportunities rather than being goal-fixated on booking.
- **What is Indicating? And Why It MUST Be Avoided** — Comprehensive discussion of how indicating creates disconnection because audiences instinctively detect inauthenticity; advocates for pursuing objectives and trusting that genuine emotion emerges naturally.
- **Why Preparing Your Audition is Ruining Your Audition (March 8)** — Argues that over-preparation locks actors into predictable choices; explains that time and information limitations in auditions are features that force authentic creative decision-making.

## Newsletter Videos

Short-form videos distributed via the TAT email newsletter. Topics range from agent relationships and character collaboration to audition philosophy. These videos emphasise agent engagement, creative experimentation, and moving beyond perfection-seeking.

- **Agents Course Newsletter Video (Nov 2024)** — Explains how actors must actively engage with agents through communication and strategy rather than assuming agents will automatically push their career forward.
- **Deliver the Ridiculous (Mar 2022)** — Uses a humorous anecdote to illustrate how memorable, unexpected character choices create lasting impressions with casting directors.
- **How To Create Characters Collaboratively** — Argues that character creation is a collaborative process involving costume, lighting, sound, and editing; emphasises listening and being part of a creative team.
- **The Perfect Audition Sucks - Here is Why** — Striving for flawless auditions creates sameness; the goal should be to be memorable and different, not technically perfect.
- **The Relationship Between the Actor and the Camera** — Discusses how the locked-off camera in auditions offers opportunity for dynamic character choices that most actors ignore, creating presence through frame positioning.
- **Three Traits of Successful Performers** — Identifies experimentation, risk management, and excitement about opportunity as the key qualities distinguishing successful actors from fearful, perfection-seeking ones.
- **Who Owns the MCU?** — Discusses character ownership in film franchises and creative control, exploring what actors can and cannot claim as their own creative contribution.

## Website Blogs

Short, practical video tips embedded in TAT blog posts. Covers foundational acting advice on creative practice, character preparation, digital age performance, and identity development. Core message: character creation is a continuous practice, not something reserved for audition day.

- **How to be a Creative Actor** — Explains that creative people must continuously practice through self-generated work; emphasises getting in front of a camera regularly to explore characters outside of auditions.
- **Video Tip - How Actors Prepare a Character** — Uses British actor Tim Healy as an example to illustrate that preparation involves experiencing the character's world (location, relationships, environment) rather than just studying dialogue.
- **Video Tip - How The Digital Age Has Changed My Day** — Discusses how casting directors now view actors through close-up framing, requiring subtle rather than big performances; bold character choices don't require exaggerated physicality.
- **Video Tip - In an Audition, Can You Talk to Yourself?** — Explains the value of breaking away from the reader to speak to oneself in an audition, creating character isolation and authenticity.
- **Video Tip - What is your Identity?** — Teaches actors to develop and recognise their character brand through experimentation; identity is created through choices, not something inherent.

## Teaching Philosophy Summary

Across all folders, the core teaching of The Audition Technique is consistent: **audition success comes from creating a distinctive, memorable character — not from perfecting a technically correct performance.** Greg Apps teaches actors to take ownership of their creative choices, embrace risk and experimentation, and measure their progress by the quality of their character work rather than by bookings or submissions.
